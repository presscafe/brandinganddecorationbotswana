## 1. Regulatory map — Botswana

### 1.1 Business formation, tax and trading
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Company registration | CIPA registration, annual returns current; trading licence for the specific activities | **[Legal requirement]** Known |
| VAT | Standard rate **14%**; compulsory registration at **P1m** turnover; returns by the 25th | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S19] |
| Corporate income tax | Was **22%**; the 2026/27 budget proposed **+3% (≈24.5%)**, with a reduced zero-rated VAT list; confirm the enacted rate and effective date | **[Legal requirement]** — **Requires validation** [S16][S17] |
| Approved manufacturing incentive | **15%** tax rate for approved manufacturing businesses; possible relevance if you move into cut-and-sew/manufacturing | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S18] |
| SPEDU incentives | 5% (first 5 years) / 10% thereafter for businesses in the Selebi-Phikwe region — a dislocation decision, not a starting point | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S18] |
| VAT modernisation | Value Added Tax (Amendment) Bill 2025: VAT on remote services, **mandatory electronic fiscal devices**; no commencement date as at the source date | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S20] |
| Vocational training levy (TRL) | Collected through the VAT system on VAT-registered persons | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S19] |

### 1.2 Product standards and imports — **the most operationally important section**
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| BOBS standards regime | Dual compulsory/voluntary standards regime administered by the Botswana Bureau of Standards; compulsory standards cover safety, health, environment and export categories | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S28] |
| Garments | **Garments are among the products subject to compulsory standards** | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S30] |
| Certificate of Conformity (CoC) | Regulations effective **1 April 2024** require regulated products to be inspected in the country of origin and to obtain a **CoC before arrival** in Botswana | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S29] |
| Implication for you | Direct-imported garments and PPE must be pre-cleared; budget time (2–6 weeks) and cost; this is a barrier most small importers ignore and a differentiation point for you | **[Recommendation]** |
| SACU customs | Botswana is in SACU: intra-SACU trade is duty-free; extra-union **clothing attracts 40–45% MFN duty** (knitted average 41.3%), plus 14% VAT; **verify HS line-by-line** | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S30][S31][S19] |
| PPE standards for end use | Mining and industrial clients will specify EN/ISO-referenced PPE (e.g. EN ISO 20471 hi-vis, EN ISO 11611/11612 for welding/thermal, EN 361 fall protection, EN 345 safety footwear). Prove conformance with test reports, not assertions | **[Industry standard]** Likely [S33] |

### 1.3 Occupational health and safety (your clients' obligations — and your sales argument)
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| No dedicated OHS Act | Botswana relies on the **Factories Act (1973)**, the **Mines, Quarries, Works and Machinery Act/Regulations**, the **Workers Compensation Act**, the Agrochemicals Act and the Radiation Protection Act rather than a single OHS Act | **[Legal requirement]** Known/Likely [S33][S34][S52] |
| Duty to provide PPE | Factories Act **s.51/s.53/s.54**: suitable protective clothing and appliances (gloves, footwear, goggles, head/face coverings) must be provided and maintained where workers are exposed to wet, injurious or offensive substances; eye protection in specified processes | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S33][S52] |
| Mining | Mines, Quarries, Works and Machinery Regulations **Reg 77** requires the use of protective equipment | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S33][S34] |
| Worker duties | Workers may not misuse safety appliances and must use provided equipment | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S52] |
| Reform watch | Confirm with the Ministry of Labour whether a dedicated OHS Act/Bill is progressing; **if enacted, PPE enforcement and demand rise materially** | **Requires validation** [S34][S35][S36] |
| Regional signal | South Africa gazetted new OHS regulations in 2025 (physical agents, noise) and **Zambia enacted OHS Act No. 16 of 2025** with mandatory workplace safety committees — the region is tightening, which is demand-positive for compliant suppliers | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S35][S36] |

### 1.4 Public procurement
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | **Public Procurement Act, 2021 (Act 24 of 2021)**, effective **14 April 2022**; regulator is the **PPRA** (which replaced PPADB) | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S21][S24] |
| Registration | Suppliers register on the **Integrated Procurement Management System (IPMS)** and purchase the correct **supplier codes**; one account per company; tax clearance must stay current | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S24] |
| Preferences | Minister sets preference schemes/margins based on **ownership, location, labour source and subcontracting**; mining procurement carries a **10% preference for locally produced supplies**; government contracts **below ~US$900,000 are reserved for citizen-owned businesses** | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S21] |
| Earlier published margins | MITI 2015 programme: **15%** preferential price margin for small locally based firms, **10%** mid, **5%** large, across **27 categories including textiles**; a 2014 mining-supplies preference of 10% was also established | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known/Likely [S22] |
| Buyer-side trend | Government prioritises locally based manufacturers where goods are available, competitively priced and BOBS-compliant; national e-procurement rollout is in progress | **[Regulatory guidance]** Known [S21] |
| Practical implication | Maintain current CIPA documents, tax clearance, audited financials, and a **standard local-content/value-addition statement** you can attach to every bid | **[Recommendation]** |

### 1.5 Data protection
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Law | **Data Protection Act, No. 18 of 2024**, assented 19 August 2024; in effect from **29 October 2024** (one guide cites commencement 14 January 2025 — verify) | **[Legal requirement]** Known/Likely [S25][S26][S27] |
| Regulator | **Information and Data Protection Commission (IDPC)**, www.idpc.org.bw | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S25][S27] |
| Registration | The Commission maintains a public register of data controllers; **no prescribed registration method** at the date of the source; sources conflict on whether registration is required — **verify directly with the IDPC** | **Requires validation** [S25][S27] |
| Breach notification | Notify the Commission **within 72 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data breach; notify data subjects where there is significant risk; keep a breach register | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S26] |
| DPO | Organisations engaged in large-scale or sensitive processing must appoint a DPO and register their contact details with the Commission | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S26] |
| Localisation | A **copy of the personal data must remain in Botswana** for the duration of processing; cross-border transfers require an adequacy decision, adequate safeguards, or reliance on statutory derogations | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S26][S27] |
| Legal bases | Processing must rest on one of six legal bases (s.26) | **[Legal requirement]** Known [S27] |
| Practical actions for this enterprise | Collect minimum PII (name, work contact, size data only where needed); store a Botswana-resident copy; encrypt backups; restrict CRM access; delete client employee data when contracts end; a short privacy notice on the storefront; appoint a named responsible person even before a formal DPO is required | **[Recommendation]** |

### 1.6 Consumer protection, IP and labour
| Item | Position | Label / confidence |
|---|---|---|

## 2. Ethics framework (business practice, not marketing)

These are operating rules, stated as policy so staff and partners can be held to them:

1. **Honesty about capability.** If a job needs a partner, say so when asked; never overstate in-house capacity or certification.
2. **No counterfeit, grey-market or untraceable safety products — ever.** If a supplier cannot provide traceability and conformity evidence, they cannot supply this enterprise. Safety failures injure workers; that is not an acceptable cost of growth.
3. **Fair pricing, transparently constructed.** Publish indicative bands; quote from a documented cost build-up; no hidden charges discovered at invoice.
4. **Clear agreements.** Written specification, written SLA, written approval for artwork, written change orders.
5. **Mutual benefit with partners and staff.** Written subcontracts, prompt payment, transparent margin splits; pay staff on time and train them — a network built on squeezing people does not survive its first busy season.
6. **No improper inducements.** No commissions, gifts or rebates to procurement staff; no bid manipulation; no collusive pricing with competitors. State this publicly and make it a sales asset in mining and government accounts.
7. **Responsible credit.** Do not extend terms that would put the business (and therefore suppliers and staff) at risk; be equally careful not to lend beyond what a client can bear.
8. **Accountability.** Every defect is investigated, logged and corrected; every missed deadline is disclosed before the client notices.
9. **Real value only.** Do not sell "sustainability" claims or local-content claims that are not evidenced. Do not market items nobody needs.
10. **Stewardship.** Minimise waste (offcuts, film, packaging), consolidate deliveries to cut fuel, and offer end-of-life take-back as the business grows.

## 5. What must be verified before any external representation

Do **not** state any of the following to a client, lender or tender authority until confirmed in writing:
- That you are "BOBS compliant" or "certified" — compliance attaches to specific products and certificates, not to a company's marketing.
- That your products "meet EN/ISO" — only with the manufacturer's test report on file for the specific SKU.
- That your local content percentage qualifies for a preference margin — only per the tender's own formula and your evidence.
- The current corporate tax rate, VAT treatment of your exact product mix, and CoC applicability of each imported line.
- That your data handling is "fully compliant" with the Data Protection Act — verify with the IDPC and counsel, then describe your controls factually.
