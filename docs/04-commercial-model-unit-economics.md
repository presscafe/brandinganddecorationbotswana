## 2. Decoration unit economics (owned capacity)

These are the margin engines. Anchored to 2026 international cost data [S42][S43][S44][S45] with Botswana labour/utility adjustments *(A)*.

| Method | Cost drivers | Direct cost/unit *(A)* | Realistic sell/unit *(A)* | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Embroidery**, left-chest logo, 6–8k stitches, 24–200 units | Thread/backing/bobbin P3–6; machine time 4–6 min; labour allocation | P6–10 | P25–45 | **65–75%** |
| **Kap embroidery** (structured cap) | As above + cap clamping, 7–9 min | P8–12 | P30–55 | **65–75%** |
| **DTF**, A4 transfer, short runs | Film/ink/powder P8–20; press 30–60 s; failures/rework allowance | P12–25 | P35–70 | **55–70%** |
| **Screen print**, 1–3 colours, 100–500 units | Screen setup P150–450 per colour (one-off), ink P4–12/unit | P6–16 + setup | P18–32 | **50–65%** |
| **Sublimation** (polyester polos, mugs, lanyards) | Paper/ink P6–15 | P10–18 | P30–60 | **55–70%** |
| **Laser engraving / UV print** (drinkware, awards) | Fixture/time driven | P8–20 | P35–90 | **60–75%** |
| **Heat-transfer vinyl** (names, numbers, Hi-Vis flash) | Vinyl P4–10 | P6–12 | P20–40 | **60–70%** |

**Machine throughput sanity check:** a single-head 15-needle machine running 5 productive hours/day at 5 minutes per logo ≈ **60 logos/hour-day**: ~300 positions/week at ~75% uptime. At P30 average sell and P8 direct cost, that machine contributes ≈ P6,600/week ≈ **P28,000/month gross contribution**. Machine cost P45k–65k landed → **payback in 2–3 months at full utilisation**, longer at 30% utilisation. *This is why you buy the machine, not the inventory.* (A — validate with real machine performance and demand.)

## 3. Worked job economics (illustrative — replace with real quotes)

### Job A — Bank staff uniform rollout: 300 golf shirts + 120 caps, embroidered
| Line | Basis | Amount (P) |
|---|---|---|
| Golf shirts, 300 @ P140 landed (SA trade account) | Product | 42,000 |
| Caps, 120 @ P65 landed | Product | 7,800 |
| Embroidery, 420 positions @ P30 sell | Decoration revenue | 12,600 |
| Decoration direct cost, 420 @ P9 | Cost | (3,780) |
| Kitting/packaging (size-labelled per staff member) | Cost | (1,800) |
| Delivery (single site, Gaborone) | Cost | (450) |
| **Revenue** | | **62,400** |
| **Direct cost** | | **(55,830)** |
| **Gross profit** | | **6,570 → 10.5%** |

⚠️ **This is the trap.** At P140 landed product cost and only P30 per embroidered position, this job is a 10% margin job — one defect batch wipes it out.
*(Consistency note: the quote engine in `store/` prices the same job at ~31.5% gross margin because it uses a P128 landed cost and a P33.60 embroidery price derived from 8,000 stitches at P4.20 per 1,000 plus digitising. That 21-point spread between the two versions of the same job is the entire point of this section: you win or die on the negotiated product cost and the decoration price, not on the sales story.)* Fixes: (1) renegotiate product tier pricing so shirts land at P118–128, not P140; (2) bundle a paid Brand Store (setup P9,500 + P2,200/month) to lift the account to ~20% blended; (3) add a programme element (annual replenishment commitment) for volume pricing; (4) never quote decorated work below a **28% floor margin including delivery**. If the client insists on the floor price, either win it on a programme commitment or decline it.

### Job B — Mining PPE issue sets: 250 sets (coverall + 2 hi-vis vests + boots + gloves + hard hat), branded
| Line | Basis | Amount (P) |
|---|---|---|
| Issue-set product cost, 250 @ P505 | Sourced (SA trade + import) | 126,250 |
| Branding (name + company logo on coveralls & vests), 250 @ P42 | Decoration revenue | 10,500 |
| Branding direct cost, 250 @ P12 | Cost | (3,000) |
| Kitting per worker + labelling + packing list | Cost | (6,250) |
| Site delivery (2 drops to remote site) | Cost | (4,500) |
| Certificate pack + compliance documentation | Cost | (1,200) |
| Payment-term cost (30-day credit, 2%) | Cost | (2,745) |
| **Revenue** | | **136,750** |
| **Direct cost** | | **(143,945)** — *negative before fixing* |

⚠️ **This exposes the second trap:** if you resell commodity PPE at 2–8% markup, a 30-day credit term makes the job value-destroying. Fixes: (1) refuse commodity-only tenders unless product margin ≥18% after trade pricing; (2) sell the *programme* (specification, kitting, scheduled delivery, reporting, certificate management) as a separately priced service at P25–60 per worker per issue; (3) require 50% deposit and progress billing; (4) price credit explicitly (2–2.5% per 30 days). **A job is only worth taking if the contribution exceeds the cost of the capital it consumes.**

### Job C — Client Brand Store + first-year order flow
| Line | Basis | Amount (P/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| Store setup (one-off) | Design, catalogue, logo vault, approval rules | 9,500 |
| Platform fee, 12 × P2,200 | Recurring | 26,400 |

Per-client store economics: ≈ P93,500 gross profit in year one for perhaps 25–35 hours of setup/support work — the highest return per hour in the entire model. Twelve such clients ≈ **P1.12m gross profit/yr** with no inventory risk and minimal marginal cost.

## 7. Working capital and cash cycle — the make-or-break

| Element | Target | Hard rule |
|---|---|---|
| Customer deposits | 50% on first order; 30% thereafter for new accounts | No exceptions for non-government new clients >P30k |
| Credit terms offered | 30 days max in Y1; 60 days only for blue-chip accounts with a facility behind them | Government work only after a facility is in place |
| Supplier terms | Negotiate 30 days with SA trade accounts; prepay import (unavoidable) | Never prepay a new importer without references and a sample |
| Inventory days | ≤35 days on buffered SKUs | Buy to order except the top 50 fast movers |
| Cash conversion cycle | **≤45 days (target), never >75 days** | If CCC exceeds 75 days for two consecutive months, stop new credit sales |
| Invoice discounting | Investigate with local banks once 3 months of clean invoices exist | Costs 2–4%/month — only worth it if gross margin >35% |

**The single most common cause of failure in this industry in Botswana is taking a large, beautifully profitable order with 60-day terms and no cash to fund it.** The business plan must include a written policy: *maximum credit exposure per client, maximum total receivables as a percentage of trailing 3-month revenue (≤35%), and a monthly receivables ageing review.*

## 8. Pricing policy (put it in writing and stick to it)

1. **Minimum gross margin floors:** decorated transactional work 32%; programme contracts 30%; stores 60% on platform fees; commodity resale 18% floor (else decline or re-source).
2. **Quote validity:** 14 days imported lines, 30 days stocked lines. Mandatory on every quote — FX and duty move.
3. **Credit is priced:** +2% per 30 days of terms.
4. **Volume tiers, not haggling:** publish 3 tiers (e.g. 25–99 / 100–499 / 500+) so sales conversations are about quantity, not discounts.
5. **Setup and artwork fees are charged** (digitising, screen setup) unless the order exceeds a stated threshold. Free setup trains clients to treat you as a hobbyist.
6. **Rush pricing:** +15–25% for ≤72-hour turnarounds, in writing.
7. **Rework policy:** free remake for our defects within 5 working days; chargeable for artwork errors approved by the client (first-article sign-off exists precisely to make this unambiguous).
8. **No retroactive discounts, no informal rebates, no "commission" payments to procurement staff.** State it publicly — it is both an ethics position and a differentiator with compliance-conscious mining and government buyers.

## 10. What would make me change this model

| If you discover… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Decoration sell prices are 30–40% below the table in §2 | Do not buy machines. Broker decoration to partners, sell programmes and stores, keep the digital layer asset-light. |
| Amrod Botswana (or a local distributor) offers you terms that beat direct import on 80% of lines | Abandon import for everything except high-run items; convert the business into a service and programme company with zero product risk. |
| Corporates won't pay for client stores | Drop the subscription thesis; monetise through programme contracts and decoration only. Re-test annually — buyer education takes time. |
| Government payment runs beyond 120 days | Exit government supply entirely except through partners or framework agreements with advance payment. |
| Mining programmes require certifications you cannot obtain | Focus on corporate and institutional segments; do not fake capability. |
| A large competitor copies the store product | Compete on service and price transparency; deepen the Brand Vault integration; move up-market into programme management, which is harder to copy. |
