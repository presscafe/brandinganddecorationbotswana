# COMPETITIVE MAP — Global, Continental, Regional, Local

**Purpose:** identify exactly where this enterprise can be structurally superior, and where it should deliberately *not* compete.
**Evidence base:** public websites, directories, industry press and research [S1]–[S52]. Competitor financials are not public; analysis is capability- and structure-based, not revenue-based. **Confidence: Likely** except where a source is cited directly.

## 1. The four competitive tiers

### Tier 1 — Global platforms and benchmarks (not competitors, but setting buyer expectations)

| Player | Model | What buyers now expect because of them | Your response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4imprint, CustomInk, Printful/Printify-type | Online-first, instant pricing, no salesperson, fast fulfilment | Instant quotes, transparent pricing, online reorder | **Match it**: the quote engine in `store/` gives instant indicative pricing; publish price bands instead of hiding them. |
| HALO Branded Solutions, BAMKO, Proforma, AIA | Distributor networks at scale; company stores; programmatic merch | "Company store" with budget control, logo vault, approval workflow | **Match it locally**: Client Brand Store product (see `03`). |
| SwagUp / managed-merch platforms | "Merch-as-a-service", kitting, fulfilment, dashboards | Onboarding kits, kitting, scheduled drops | **Match it for programmes**: PPE issue sets and kitting are the same muscle. |
| PromoStandards ecosystem (500+ suppliers), commonsku, SAGE, OrderMyGear, BrightStores | Industry data standards and workflow software | Machine-readable product data, electronic POs, inventory checks | **Be first in Botswana to run a structured catalogue** [S40][S41]. |

**Key insight:** no global platform will build Botswana-specific local content, BOBS compliance handling, or PPRA-ready tender documentation. Their absence is your protected space — for now.

### Tier 4 — Botswana print/branding micro-operators (your execution competition)

Earnest Printing & Branding (Gaborone, in-house print + PPE + workwear + gifts + website), Artistic Printz & Embroidery, Mindota, Prime Grafix, Eagle Tracks, BW House of T-shirts, Urban Zebra Tees, Balayi (Maun), Mason-m Advertising, plus an unknown number of informal embroiderers and DTF operators. Sources: [S49].

**Their pattern:** owner-operated, One-Machine-One-Person, cash-flow constrained, quality varies by operator, no compliance documentation, cannot scale a 500+ unit rollout across multiple sites, no online ordering, no client portal, no Tender-ready paperwork.

> **Strategic conclusion #2:** these micro-operators are not competitors to be crushed — they are **your partner network**. They already have machines, customers and local presence in towns you cannot afford to staff. Your advantage is process, compliance, digital front-end, and the ability to win the big jobs they cannot. A vetted network with written SLAs, QC checklists and scorecards converts fragmented capacity into contracted capacity.

## 3. The wedge — one sentence, then proof

> **You win where a job is too complex for a micro-shop, too local for a South African supplier, and too branded for a commodity PPE distributor.**

That is not a slogan; it is a segment definition you can test. Jobs fitting that definition in Botswana include: a 600-unit branded uniform rollout across three towns; a bank's executive gifting programme with approval workflows; a mine's quarterly PPE issue-set replenishment with size matrices and laundering coordination; a bank/NGO event kit delivered to 800 delegates in 10 days; a school uniform tender requiring local content documentation.

## 4. Positioning and messaging

**Positioning (recommended, singular):**
> Programme-grade branding partner. You specify it; we source it, decorate it, deliver it — with compliance paperwork and a portal that makes reordering effortless.

**Proof points to build (in priority order):**
1. **On-time record** — publish a monthly measured on-time % (target ≥95%).
2. **Defect rate** — publish a measured ≤2% rework rate with a written rework policy.
3. **Compliance pack** — CoC, certificates per SKU, MSDS for chemicals, PPE standards references, per order.
4. **Local value addition statement** — a standard one-page document quantifying Botswana labour and processing content, ready for tender evaluations.
5. **Case studies** with named clients (start with the first five orders, even discounted).
6. **Price transparency** — published indicative bands by quantity tier, which no local competitor does.

**Naming and digital identity (decisions to take, low cost):**
- Keep **Branding & Decoration Botswana** as the legal/trading name (it is descriptive and SEO-friendly).
- Consider a short platform brand for the technology layer (e.g. a distinct name for the Client Brand Store). Recommendation: keep one brand, two product names — "Programme" and "Store" — rather than inventing a separate tech brand. Two brands double the marketing cost for no gain at this scale.
- Domain candidates (availability **unverified** — check before purchase): `brandinganddecoration.co.bw`, `brandingbotswana.co.bw`, `bdorchestra.co.bw`. Verify `.co.bw` registration rules with BOCRA-accredited registrars.

## 5. Competitive threats and how each is neutralised

| Threat | Probability | Impact | Neutralisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amrod Botswana opens a local decoration/advisory service for *distributors* | Medium | Low | Stay their best reseller — volume, reliable payment, accurate artwork. Their trade-only policy protects you. |
| SA distributor sets up a Botswana branch with local branding | Medium | High | Move fast on programme contracts (3-year terms), build switching costs via client stores and logo vaults. |
| Local PPE distributor hires a good brander and copies the model | High | Medium | Your moat is the *system* (quote engine, routing, QC, scorecards, store platform), not the machine. Copying a machine takes 3 weeks; copying an operating system takes years. |
| Price war on commodity lines | High | Medium | Refuse to lead with commodity lines; compete on total delivered cost and risk reduction, not unit price. |
| Government/parastatal payment delays | High | High | 50% deposits on first orders; no-government-job-without-facility rule; invoice discounting; portfolio cap of 25% revenue per client. |
| Counterfeit or non-conforming PPE entering your supply chain | Medium | Severe | Approved-supplier list, certificate-per-SKU file, sample retention, written supplier warranty, immediate disclosure if a defect is found. |
| Talent poaching / key-person loss | Medium | High | SOPs, cross-training, documented artwork and pricing, non-compete and confidentiality clauses in employment contracts (verify enforceability in Botswana with counsel). |

## 6. The moat — what compounds

1. **Client Brand Vaults.** Logo files, pantones, size matrices, approval chains and budget rules per client. Once embedded, switching cost is a procurement headache nobody wants.
2. **Verified supplier and certificate database.** Which SKU, which certificate, which test report, which supplier, at what landed cost — an asset that improves with every order.
3. **Partner network with scorecards.** Contracted capacity in six towns, with measured reliability. Capital-free expansion.
4. **Owned data:** Botswana branded-supply price index, tender radar, product catalogue with structural data. Nobody else is collecting it.
5. **Tender documentation track record.** Each submitted bid teaches you the evaluation criteria; each win becomes a reference.
6. **SOP library.** Turns talent into process; lets you clone capability across towns.
